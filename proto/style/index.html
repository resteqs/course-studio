Object rules

  • One camera

    Every object uses the same orthographic view, turned 25° and looking down 36°, so the top, front and right sides always show.

  • One light, three tones

    Light comes from the top left. Up-facing walls are the lightest tone, the front is the base colour, right-facing walls are the darkest. No gradients, no transparency.

  • Ink, never black

    Each object is outlined in its own darkest colour, on the silhouette and where two tones meet. Flat faces carry no extra lines.

  • Shadows on one floor

    Every vertex is cast onto the floor along one light, 0.4 units of throw per unit of height. Collectibles hover 4 units up; chests, ice and nodes touch their shadow. In the toolbar, objects drop the shadow so it does not read as a second icon.

  • Lesson nodes

    A puck seen from above: a 34 by 24 top over an 8-unit lip, 2 units when pressed. The base stays put, so a press moves only the top and shortens the shadow.

  • Fixed colours

    XP yellow, gems pink, streak orange, freezes ice blue, mistakes red, in every palette. If an accent is close to Done gold in both lightness and hue, the current node switches to a neutral face with an accent ring.

What we take from whom

  • Duolingo

    Fixed colours for game objects and chunky controls with a bottom lip, on a dark canvas instead of its white one.

  • Brilliant

    Restraint and precise geometry, with more colour than its line art so rewards feel like rewards.

  • Linear

    Themes generated from base, accent and contrast in a perceptual colour space, without the dense productivity layout.

  • Vercel (Geist)

    A strict neutral scale where typography does most of the work, warmed slightly so it is not stark.

  • Kurzgesagt

    Bold flat colour and silhouettes that read at a glance. We skip the glows.

  • Carrot Weather and Monument Valley

    A mascot who talks to you, looks worth collecting, and one light direction for the whole world. Kubo stays kind where Carrot is snarky.
