Three open choices, tried for real

Each prototype runs the same task in two or three directions. Variant 1 is how the app behaves today. Try each one at full size before reading the table, switch with Alt+1/2/3, and note what you noticed before you pick: one, a mix, one more round, or none. The motion slider and sound toggle sit in the bar on top. Everything outside the part being judged is rough on purpose, and the lesson text is written for the prototypes, not taken from the course.

Axis: how an answer is committed and how feedback arrives.

Answer three checks. Get the first wrong once, the second wrong twice, and press Check with the number box empty.

VariantPlace on the axisRight whenCosts
1 TodayMixed: numbered options commit on tap, lettered ones need Check; feedback is an inline cardYou want to keep what learners already knowTwo rules to learn, Check can be pressed again on the same wrong pick, empty Check does nothing
2 Check + sheetEvery check is pick, then Check; a sheet rises from the bottom with the resultFeedback should feel like a moment, and a miss should come back laterOne extra tap per question; the sheet covers the bottom of the screen
3 Tap + explainA tap is the answer; wrong options explain themselves in placeSpeed and flow matter most, and reasons should sit next to the wrong ideaNo take-backs on a slip; the explanation pushes content down

Finishing a lesson

Open →

Axis: how much ceremony and information the finish carries, and in what order.

You just earned 45 XP, got 4 of 5 checks right first try in 3:42, finished the daily quest and reached a 6 day streak. Go to the end. Ask what you feel, what you know now, and whether you want the next lesson.

VariantPlace on the axisRight whenCosts
1 TodayShort: a title and the completion bonus, then streak and quest screensLearners chain lessons quickly and want to move onShows +15 XP when the lesson paid 45; nothing about what was learned
2 Count-up + chestHigh ceremony: stat tiles count up, the quest chest opens, the streak flipsThe finish is the main reward loop and retention is the goalThree screens and about ten seconds per lesson; the learning itself is not named
3 Recap firstOne screen: what you can now do, unit progress, compact rewards, next lessonMastery and the next step should outweigh the lootLess spectacle; needs a short “you can now” line written for every lesson

Course home

Open →

Axis: how many things compete for the first click.

Day 12, a 5 day streak, 30 of 50 quest XP, one Boost saved. In five seconds, what would you click? Then find the next lesson, the quest and card review. Repeat at the 390px phone width.

VariantPlace on the axisRight whenCosts
1 TodayEverything visible: three columns, two Start buttons, stats, quest, Boost, leaderboard, achievementsPower users who want every number on one screenSeveral equal calls to action; “Start” means two different things
2 One hero + Today railThe path is the page, the next lesson owns the only big button; one quiet rail for todayThe path should stay the mental model and the rewards should feel like objectsAchievements and stats move to the profile
3 Daily sessionOne planned session (lesson plus cards) with one button; the path becomes a stripReturning and ADHD-prone learners who need the decision made for themLess sense of a journey; free browsing is a step further away

These are throwaway prototypes for a decision. None of this code is meant to be merged as it is. Variants 2 and 3 share one visual direction to judge alongside the choices: less chrome, no accent rails or cards inside cards, and each unit owning a property colour that repeats on its tiles.
